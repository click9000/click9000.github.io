Why five petals?

Venus's year is about 225 Earth days, Earth's is 365. Their ratio sits remarkably close to 13 : 8 — in the time Earth makes eight trips around the Sun, Venus makes almost exactly thirteen. Every conjunction, when Venus slips between Earth and the Sun, happens a little further along the zodiac than the last.

The two planets meet 13 − 8 = 5 times in each eight-year cycle, and those five meeting points land evenly spaced around the Sun — the vertices of a slow pentagram. The chord drawn between the planets sweeps through every in-between position too, filling the pentagram out into the rounded, five-petaled rose you see above. Eight years later the pattern closes and begins again.

It is a near-resonance, not an exact one — the true ratio is 1.6257, a hair off 13/8 = 1.625. Switch on True periods above and let it run: the flower no longer quite closes, and the whole rose slowly rotates, precessing through the sky over centuries.
